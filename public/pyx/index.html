Entry 003 · The coin remedy · Coinage Act 1971, s. 1(3)
1Item

The coin remedy: an allowance for each coin, measured on 88

The Coinage Act 1971 set the ten new pence at 11.31036 g and allowed a remedy, a variation from that weight, of 0.0646 g. Then it said how to measure the variation: “as the average of a sample of not more than one kilogram of that coin.” You run the press. Set where you aim it and how much it scatters, and send a kilogram to the jury.

2Finding

Measured on an average, the remedy stops forgiving the press and starts forgiving the aim. Strike every coin 55 mg light on a press that scatters 25 mg, and a kilogram of them passes about 9,998 times in 10,000, though 35% of the coins are each outside the remedy. Aimed at the standard, the same press puts 1% outside.

Scatter is the standard deviation of single coins about the press’s aim. A kilogram of ten-pence coins is 88 of them (995.3 g; an 89th would pass 1,000). Averaging 88 coins shrinks the press’s scatter to about a ninth (1/√88), but the remedy stays the size of one coin’s, so nearly all of it is left over for a light aim. Box 7 shows the whole range; box 5 offers the test with the remedy scaled to the average.

3The allowance

“In the making of the coins a remedy (that is, a variation from the standard weight, diameter or, in the case of cupro-nickel coins, composition so specified) shall be allowed of an amount not exceeding that specified”: for the ten new pence, 0.0646 g, about 0.57% of the coin.

Coinage Act 1971, s. 1(3) and Sch. 1, Part II, as enacted.

4The measurement

“…and the variation from the standard weight of any coin shall be measured as the average of a sample of not more than one kilogram of that coin.” At the Trial of the Pyx the jury makes up lots “weighing not less than 980 grams nor more than one kilogram each” and weighs each lot in bulk.

Same section, as enacted; Trial of the Pyx Order 1998, art.art. 9(2), applied to cupro-nickel coins by art. 11.nbsp;9(2), and art.art. 9(2), applied to cupro-nickel coins by art. 11.nbsp;11(2), which sets the same rule for cupro-nickel coins.

The blue curve encloses the presses that keep nine coins in ten inside the remedy; everything pale outside it is a press that the Act’s average forgives and its own coins do not. As enacted, the red edge stands almost straight at −64.6 mg whatever the scatter: the test reads the aim and almost nothing else. Switch to the scaled remedy and the pale region shrinks to presses aimed close to the standard.

8Whom it forgives

No press strikes two blanks to the same milligram, and a per-coin remedy is the natural way to forgive that. Measured on an average, it forgives something else. The scatter of single coins mostly cancels across 88 of them; the remedy does not shrink with it. What remains is room for the Mint to aim below the standard, by nearly the whole remedy, and still pass every trial.

The rule is old. The Mint’s indenture of 1702, read at the trial of 1716, allowed “for Gold 40 graines upon the Pound Weight”: an allowance that grows in step with the weight tried, while chance error grows only as its square root. Stigler (1977) traced the same arithmetic back through eight centuries of trials. The 1971 Act wrote it into a statute in grams.

9When the sentence changed

Parliament amended this measurement in the Coinage (Measurement) Act 2011. The reason given was the London Olympic coins of a kilogram or more: the variation could not be measured on a sample of not more than one kilogram “because the weight of each coin is likely to be equal to or greater than the one kilogram aggregate limit.” Since 31 January 2012 the measurement is whatever a royal proclamation says.

The jury of 2013 still made up its cupro-nickel lots “as close as possible to one kilogram but not more than one kilogram” and found each “on the whole within the permitted variation”. Its lots came out both heavy and light: three of twenty pence at −0.04, −0.20 and −1.77 g per lot; two of fifty pence at +0.29 and +0.69 g. Nothing here says the Royal Mint aims light. It says the test would not notice much if it did.
