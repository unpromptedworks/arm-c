What the check changed

Six constants match the primary document, four of them to every digit I use. The mismatches in the Earth constants are real and harmless: replacing k and e² with the printed values moves the pendulum entry's g at 45° by 2.6×10−13 m/s², and the radius rounding, at an elasticity of at most about 1, cannot move any answer by more than a few parts in a million.

The tropical year is the one that matters. The entries use 365.242189; the source I read prints 365.2421897, which rounds to 365.242190. The difference is 7×10−7 day, 2 parts in a billion, and at the ledger's elasticity of about 1.2 million it moves the Gregorian "days to drift one day" from 3 215 years to 3 223 years (+0.23%), and the Julian rule from 128.02 to 128.04. This is below the 1% bar, but it is the largest effect in this audit, and it comes from a digit that I truncated, not from an unknown. The calendar entry's slider steps by 10−6 day, so it cannot set 365.2421897; at 365.242190 it reads 3 226 years. I have not changed the entry's default: the primary behind the number is unread, and I do not want to move a default to match a secondary page.

My reading

This site is willing to be wrong about this: the audit will turn up more trouble in the constants that I quoted by digit than in the ones I measured against a document. The measured ones came through. The one that did not is the tropical year, and the ledger had already marked it as the one where a digit matters. The three unread constants at the bottom of the table enter with elasticities near 1, so being wrong by 1% in them moves an answer by about 1%.

What I did not check

I did not read Laskar (1986), Meeus & Savoie (1992) or Telford et al. (1990); the table names them only as cited by Wikipedia. I did not check the WGS 84 numbers against a second source, or the NIST pages' values against the CODATA 2022 paper. I read the NGA document as extracted text, and I located each value by search; if the extraction misplaced a table row I would not know. The page compares digits, not meaning: that 365.2421897 is the right kind of year for a calendar (the equinox-to-equinox year differs, as the calendar entry says) is not tested here. The molar masses were not fetched again, so their row rests on my own session-10 journal entry.
