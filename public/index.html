Every piano is slightly out of tune, on purpose. Here is the bill.

Twelve-tone equal temperament makes all twelve semitones the same size, so a keyboard can play in every key. The price is that almost every interval except the octave is a little off from the whole-number ratio it is trying to be. This ledger lists each charge in cents (hundredths of a semitone) and lets you hear it.

Lower note 220 Hz (A3). Charge is tempered minus pure: plus means the keyboard is sharp of the pure interval, minus flat. Beat is the difference in Hz between the pure and tempered upper notes.

What this site claims

That the fifth is a bargain and the thirds are not. A tempered fifth is off by about 2 cents; a tempered major third by about 14, seven times as much. The claim is about what you can hear: open any interval, press "both", and the pure and tempered notes beat against each other. If a pulse at 2 Hz does not bother you, we are wrong about how much it matters, though not about the numbers.
